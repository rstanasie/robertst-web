## The Council {#the-council}

Zeus had counted the gifts and found that men had none. No fur against the
winter, no speed, no claw. What was left in the sack when the animals were
finished was nothing at all, and the gods agreed that this was fitting.

Prometheus did not agree. He had made them, after a fashion, out of river clay
and his own stubbornness, and a maker is never impartial.

## The Theft {#the-theft}

He went up at the hour when the forge was banked and the halls were loud, and he
took a coal no larger than a seed. He carried it in the pith of a fennel stalk,
which burns slowly and without smoke, and he walked down out of heaven at an
ordinary pace, because a man in a hurry is a man worth stopping.

By morning there was light in the valleys that had not been lit from above.

## The Rock {#the-rock}

They took him east, past where the maps give up, and pinned him to the rock with
a spike through the chest. An eagle came at noon. What it ate grew back in the
night, so that the punishment could be administered again the following day, and
the following, without ever being finished.

This is the part of the story that is usually shortened. It should not be. The
point of it is the repetition.

## What He Did Not Say {#what-he-did-not-say}

He knew a thing Zeus wanted: the name of the child who would unseat him. In
thirty thousand years of the eagle he never said it, and that silence — not the
fire, not the theft — is what the gods could not forgive.

Fire only warms a room. A secret kept is a kingdom held hostage.
