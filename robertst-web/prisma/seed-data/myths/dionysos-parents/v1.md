We find ourselves high on Mount Olympus.

I think she's the one, she's different, thinks Zeus as he watches a woman walking around the castle of Cadmeia, his eyes dry. Such grace in her walk, by god!

Zeus considers transmutation (something that usually works), but for some reason decides to approach her in his human form. As Princess Semele stares at the sky, he godly-appears in the castle garden, taking the risk of acting like a human being.

"Good evening Semele, what a beautiful sky isn't it?" he says as he approaches her from behind.

"Oh! Hello, stranger, yes indeed."

"I'm Zeus by the way."

"Zeus? Rings a bell... do you see those stars over there, Zeus? That's Orion, the giant."

"Yes, he was really tall. My daughter Artemis killed the guy. She was in love with him."

She tilts her head slightly and peers at the sky.

"Interesting."

Zeus strokes his beard.

"Look, I think you're beautiful and I like the way you walk."

"Oh my god, oh my god! You've been stalking me?"

"No, no, no!"

"Fuck!" says Zeus to himself as he turns slightly and starts consulting his Transmutations manual.

"Just joking, dear Zeus! I'm really flattered. How are things at the Olympus?" she says, giving him a playful punch on the shoulder, almost knocking the manual out of his sweaty fingers.

"Oh well, it's going really well, we won the battle against the Titans a couple of decades ago and we've been reflecting since then, sort of like a retreat of gods, on the victory to see what could be improved and I... well, during this reflection I've seen you here and I..."

"So you've been stalking me."

"No, no, no!"

Semele jumps onto Zeus and doesn't stop at kissing him.

Godly pregnancy is served, bullseye.

Back to the Olympus. Zeus and Apollo chat away off to one side.

"By god! No transmutation? What did you tell her?"

"Well, she was talking about constellations and all that shit and I told her, 'Your grace deserves a place among the stars,' that was it, she melted!"

"You dirty dog!" says Apollo. "Nice catch!"

"I'm going to see her now."

"Again? But that was barely a week ago!"

"Well I already told her so, talk to you later!"

As Zeus is about to perform his god-like space travel:

"Zeus! You're leaving again?" calls Hera from another room.

"Oh honey, this is sort of a retreat I'm doing... since the Titans' war, to reflect on what to improve and stuff..."

"Alone?"

"Yes honey, you see I discovered this thing called meditation and it's good, and now I think I..."

"You think?"

"Exactly! I've been doing it, yeah."

Zeus scratches his beard.

"Ok, enjoy!" says Hera with a smile.

"Thank you honey!" echoes out as Zeus leaves the place.

Hera grabs the Olympus telescope and starts following Zeus.

He stops on an empty island and strolls through its scraggly woods; then jumps to another and does the same thing again; and then again one more time. Hours fly by and Hera is still watching.

"I guess he's really reflecting on that stuff, that's good."

Just before putting away the telescope, she sees Zeus jumping to another place.

Cadmeia.

"I knew it!" says Hera under her breath.

"Aphrodite! Bring the popcorn!"

Zeus appears at Semele's window.

"You're late," Semele says.

"I'm sorry, dear Semele, I had to do God of the Olympus stuff, you know."

"I can imagine."

"Yes, it was this problem with... you know, the Titans' war, something happened that I was really trying to fix and I..."

"Interesting."

"Can I come in?"

"I'm really tired tonight, can we move it to another day?"

"Of course! Let me know when you're available!"

Semele turns off the light.

Zeus rubs his beard.

"Well, I'm leaving now!" whispers Zeus.

A sleepy sigh.

"Good night!"

The bedsheets rustle.

One god-hour later, which could mean several days in human time, Hera godly-descends to Cadmeia to pay Semele a visit.

"Good evening, dear Semele, you look outstanding darling, look at your skin!"

"I bow to you, glorious Hera, goddess of family, queen of the Olympus, the most beautiful and worthwhile wife of all wives worth having, with such wisdom, grace, breath..."

"Thank you! Thank you for your kind words, dear Semele." She gives a godly bow.

"So between us darling, I know we haven't talked in a while, like, never? The Olympus is a mess, too much work darling, anyways, who's the lucky man?"

"It is Zeus, glorious Hera, goddess of fam..."

"Oh really! What a surprise! But... how could that happen? My dear husband has been very much at home lately, fixing some wobbly chairs and window frames... I don't know dear Semele, are you sure that he's the father?"

"Well, he looked exactly like that statue."

Semele points to a statue of Zeus in the garden.

"Just the beard wasn't that big in person," continues Semele.

They both stare at the statue of Zeus. Semele squints; Hera bites her lip.

"Well, that's trending now, maybe it wasn't him," says Hera.

"How can one be absolutely certain of such a thing?"

"Well, do you really want to know?"

"Of course, how come not!?"

"But do you really need to know?" Hera continues as she looks deeply into Semele's eyes, her eyes growing wider and her voice growing graver.

"I don't want to anymore, to be honest," replies Semele gently.

"I'll tell you anyways, the only way to be certain is to make this man who calls himself Zeus show you his true self. Zeus's true self is impossible to look away from and leaves no room for doubt."

"Interest..."

Hera continues: "Don't speak, I know what you're thinking, and I don't need your reasons..."

"Oh my god, oh my god! Thank you very much, glorious Hera, goddess of..."

"My pleasure, darling!"

Several days pass for Semele, several seconds for the gods. Zeus keeps discussing the Titans problem:

"So she kicked you out of her place?" asks Apollo.

"No, man, she didn't kick me out, she was just tired."

"Tired of what? Of your beard?"

"I'm telling you man, she's different, look at her."

Semele, while putting on her pyjamas, glances at the sky for a moment.

"Hey, she's looking up, I think it's a sign!" whispers Zeus.

"Yes, a sign of tiredness."

"Look, she did that again! I have to go!"

"Ugh, you're such a chihuahua."

In the next blink, Zeus is beside Semele.

"Good night, dear Zeus, how is carpentry going?"

"Good night, beautiful Semele, well, I... you know wood is not really my domain of knowledge."

"I'm sure, dear Zeus. Regarding that, I really need something from you, as the parent of my unborn child, but I'm not sure if you will be able to help me."

"By god! Anything you wish, gorgeous Semele, do you need a new castle? Consider it done; do you need help in some goofy-ass human's war? I will make your enemies perish; do you need fashion advice? I know a guy. I swear by the River Styx! You wish, I provide."

"Oh my god! Oh my god! Great! Well... I would love you to show me your true self!"

Zeus stops stroking his beard.

"Fuck."

Zeus returns to Olympus, grabs the lightning and thunder, brushes his beard, and suddenly he's floating in the essence of power, immersed in a tornado of god-like energy. You could feel the breeze from miles away, like a strong and prolonged fart.

He returns to Cadmeia and enters the castle, almighty and stuffed with the power of his brushed beard.

As he steps through the gates, the whole building starts trembling, falling apart as if it were made of straw. The roof collapses, the floors crumble, and flames consume the castle.

Like a fucking mortal she forgot she was, dear Semele stands there contemplating, blinded by admiration for such power and such a beard, as her flesh and bones are crushed.

Zeus grabs her like a knight in deadly shining armour saving a damsel in irreversible distress and, with surgical precision, extracts the baby from inside the excited and barely breathing Semele.

Then, as if the guy had a medical degree, he places the premature baby inside his thigh.

Not bad for a carpenter trainee.

The Styx oath is something that, more often than not, makes even gods think twice. Breaking this oath would condemn Zeus to spend one year in Tartarus and another nine years without setting foot on Olympus.
