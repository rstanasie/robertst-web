## The Workshop {#the-workshop}

They were prisoners of a building his father had designed. Daedalus had made the
labyrinth so well that he could not find his own way out of it, which is the
kind of joke the gods enjoy.

Feathers came cheap on that island. Wax came cheaper.

## The Instruction {#the-instruction}

Not too high, his father said, or the sun will soften the wax. Not too low, or
the sea will weight the feathers. Fly in the narrow band between the two things
that will kill you, and do not look at either.

He was a boy. He had been told to be careful his entire life, by a man whose
carefulness had built the prison.

## The Climb {#the-climb}

Somewhere over the water he found that the wings held. That is the part nobody
dwells on: there was a stretch of the morning in which it simply worked, and he
was the first person who had ever done it, and it was better than anything.

Then he went up. Of course he went up.

## The Water {#the-water}

Daedalus flew on to Sicily and built a temple and hung the wings in it, and lived
a long time, and was careful.

The sea is still called after the boy.
