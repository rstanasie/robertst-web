## Twice Born {#twice-born}

His mother asked to see his father as he really was, having been advised to by a
woman who was not a woman, and Zeus, who had sworn to give her anything, could
not refuse. He arrived as lightning. Semele burned where she stood.

What was taken out of the fire was not finished yet. Zeus opened his own thigh
and carried the rest of the term himself, which is the only birth in the whole
literature where the father does any of the work.

## The Vine {#the-vine}

The gift he brought was not wine. Wine was only the vehicle. What he brought was
the discovery that a person can be got out of themselves for an evening and
returned more or less intact — that the self is a thing you can put down.

Every city that took the gift also took the terms, and the terms were never
written anywhere.

## The Stranger {#the-stranger}

He comes from the east, or from the sea, or from further off than either. The
stories are careful about this: he is always arriving, never from here. A young
man on the road, unarmed, smiling, asking to be let in.

The god of the vine is also the god of the outsider at the gate, and those two
are the same story told from opposite ends.

## What Pentheus Learned {#what-pentheus-learned}

The king of Thebes decided the cult was a disorder and the god a fraud, and had
him arrested, which went the way it always goes.

He was taken up the mountain to see for himself, and the women of his own city
tore him apart without recognising him, his mother first among them, carrying
his head down into the town in triumph and asking to be praised for it.

The lesson usually drawn is about hubris. It is really about what a place does
to itself when it decides that some part of what people need is not permitted.
